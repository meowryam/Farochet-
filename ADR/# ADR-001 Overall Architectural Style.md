# ADR-001: Overall Architectural Style

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:** 
Raazia’s Customer Inquiry and Custom Order Management System requires a centralized digital application to manage product catalogs, inquiries, custom orders, and varied pricing structures. The identified Architecture Significant Requirements (ASRs) include a self-contained system with manual record-keeping and no external payment or courier integrations (ASR-05 in [ASRs identification.docx](../References/ASRs%20identification.docx); [PRD §10, Constraints and Exclusions](../PRD.md#10-constraints-and-exclusions)). Key architectural drivers include role-based access control between Raazia and her customers ([PRD NFR-01](../PRD.md#nfr-01-basic-access-control)), keeping inquiry data separate from confirmed orders ([PRD FR-06](../PRD.md#fr-06-inquiry-history-retention)), maintaining independent production, payment, and delivery statuses ([PRD FR-14](../PRD.md#fr-14-independent-order-payment-and-delivery-statuses), [PRD NFR-03](../PRD.md#nfr-03-essential-data-integrity)), and preventing negotiated or discounted prices from overwriting standard catalogue prices ([PRD FR-12](../PRD.md#fr-12-order-specific-pricing-and-price-change-records), [PRD FR-03](../PRD.md#fr-03-public-discount-management-and-calculation)).

**Alternatives Considered:**
1. **Microservices Architecture:** Decomposing the system into independent services (e.g., Inventory Service, Order Service, User Service) with isolated databases. The project alternatives document notes that this adds operational overhead, data-consistency challenges, and network latency not justified for this small business application ([Exploration of Architectural Alternatives](../References/Exploration%20of%20Architectural%20Alternatives%2Cjustificationand%20interfaces.docx), “Microservices Architecture”).
2. **Multi-Tier (Layered) Architecture with MVC:** Separating the application into Presentation, Business Logic, and Data Access tiers, while using MVC to organize presentation concerns ([Exploration of Architectural Alternatives](../References/Exploration%20of%20Architectural%20Alternatives%2Cjustificationand%20interfaces.docx), “Multi-Tier (Layered) Architecture” and “Model-View-Controller”).

**Decision:**
We will adopt a **Multi-Tier (Layered) Architecture** consisting of three separate layers: Presentation, Business Logic, and Data Access. The **Model-View-Controller (MVC) pattern is used within the Presentation Layer only** to organize Views, Controllers, and presentation-level data/state. MVC does not replace or absorb the separate Business Logic or Data Access layers.

**Rationale:**
This architectural style directly addresses the system's ASRs without introducing unnecessary complexity:
* **Self-Contained Edges:** A unified monolithic multi-tier system aligns with the requirement for a manual, self-contained application without external gateways ([PRD §10, Constraints and Exclusions](../PRD.md#10-constraints-and-exclusions); [Exploration of Architectural Alternatives](../References/Exploration%20of%20Architectural%20Alternatives%2Cjustificationand%20interfaces.docx), “Justification Based on ASRs”).
* **Security & Access Control (NFR-01):** The multi-tier structure supports centralized security. The centralized Controller Middleware at the Presentation request boundary acts as a gatekeeper for role-based authorization ([PRD NFR-01](../PRD.md#nfr-01-basic-access-control); [ADR-004](./%23%20ADR-004%20Centralized%20Access%20Control%20and%20Authorization%20Boundary.md)).
* **Data Integrity & Consistency (FR-06, FR-12, FR-14):** A centralized Data Access tier supports the separation and persistence of distinct records. Business Logic handles pricing rules and inquiry/order separation, while Data Access maps these concepts to relational persistence ([PRD FR-06](../PRD.md#fr-06-inquiry-history-retention), [PRD FR-12](../PRD.md#fr-12-order-specific-pricing-and-price-change-records), [PRD FR-14](../PRD.md#fr-14-independent-order-payment-and-delivery-statuses); [ADR-002](./%23%20ADR-002%20Data%20Persistence%20and%20Separation%20for%20Pricing%20and%20Inquiries.md)).

**Trade-offs / Consequences:**
* **Pros:** The architecture supports testability, separation of responsibilities, and maintainability ([architecture.md, §2.1](../architecture.md#21-architectural-goals); [Exploration of Architectural Alternatives](../References/Exploration%20of%20Architectural%20Alternatives%2Cjustificationand%20interfaces.docx), “Multi-Tier (Layered) Architecture”).
* **Cons:** A centralized database may become a bottleneck at very large scales; the project alternatives document considers that concern insignificant for the stated small-business scope ([Exploration of Architectural Alternatives](../References/Exploration%20of%20Architectural%20Alternatives%2Cjustificationand%20interfaces.docx), “Multi-Tier (Layered) Architecture”).
